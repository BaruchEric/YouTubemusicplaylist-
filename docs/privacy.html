Privacy policy

Last updated 26 November 2026

CrateSync keeps your music library in your own browser. It has no database of its own. It talks to Google only when you ask it to: to sign you in, to find your songs on YouTube, to push a playlist there, to sync with your Google Drive, or to look up song details.

It shows no ads, runs no analytics and sells nothing to anyone.

Who runs CrateSync

CrateSync is a personal project. Its developer runs one copy at music.beric.ca for private use, behind a sign-in. The source code is public. Questions about this policy go to cratesync@beric.ca.

What stays on your device

CrateSync saves these in your browser's storage for the site, on the device you use:

  • The list of your songs with their tags, cover art, file names and folder paths.
  • Copies of the audio files you add one by one, drop onto the page or download from Google Drive, so they still play after a reload. A browser that cannot keep a reference to a folder, such as Safari, Firefox and the browsers on phones and tablets, also stores a copy of each song in a folder you scan.
  • In Chrome or Edge on a computer, for a folder you pick with the folder chooser, a reference to that folder and the list of the songs found in it. CrateSync keeps no copy of those songs. It reads each one from the folder when you play it.
  • Your playlists, smart playlists and the history of your syncs.
  • Play counts, skips and a play history of up to 2,000 entries.
  • Your settings, and a name for the device.
  • The YouTube video ID you matched to a song and the time YouTube last confirmed that video, the ID and link of a YouTube playlist you pushed to, the IDs of the videos CrateSync added to it, and the time of the last push.
  • Which songs Match YouTube searched for and when, for 30 days, so it does not spend a search on the same song twice.

None of this is sent to the CrateSync server. It leaves your device only in the cases below.

Signing in with Google

Sign-in runs on Firebase Authentication, a Google service. When you sign in, Google gives CrateSync your name, email address, profile picture and an account ID. Firebase keeps an account record with those details and your sign-in times. CrateSync uses the email address to show which account is connected and to decide who may use the song lookups.

Firebase App Check with reCAPTCHA Enterprise checks that a sign-in comes from the real app. reCAPTCHA collects device and browser details under the Google Privacy Policy.

YouTube

CrateSync uses YouTube API Services to find the YouTube video for a song and to send a playlist to your YouTube account. It asks Google for access to your YouTube account when you click Push to YouTube, or when you start a search or a playlist read in Match YouTube, and at no other time.

A push does five things with that access:

  • It creates a private playlist on your account when the playlist has none yet.
  • It reads the entries of the playlist it pushes to. It asks only for each entry's ID, its video ID and its position.
  • It adds the videos matched to your songs.
  • It moves a video that is out of order.
  • It removes a video, and only one that an earlier push from CrateSync added.

Match YouTube does two things with it, and only the one you pick:

  • It searches YouTube for a song. The search sends the song's artist and title to YouTube and gets back up to five videos, each with its ID, title and channel name.
  • It reads a playlist you give it the link of, which can be your liked videos. It asks for each video's ID, title, channel name and position.

CrateSync shows you the videos it found next to your songs. It saves the video ID of each match you apply. It does not save the titles or the channel names.

A saved video ID is checked again after 30 days. The next time you push a playlist or use Match YouTube, CrateSync asks YouTube whether the videos saved more than 30 days ago still exist. That question sends the video IDs and nothing else. A song whose video is gone loses its match, and CrateSync tells you how many did.

CrateSync does not read your watch history, your subscriptions, your comments or your channel details, and it reads no playlist you did not give it. It does not download audio or video from YouTube.

The access token from Google stays in the page's memory. CrateSync never writes it to storage, drops it after 50 minutes, and loses it when you reload the page. CrateSync gets no token that would let it act while you are away.

From YouTube, CrateSync keeps only the playlist ID and the video IDs listed under "What stays on your device". It reads the playlist again on every push. To delete a saved video ID, clear the match on the song or delete the song.

When a song has no audio file on your device, CrateSync can play its match in YouTube's embedded player, and it shows video thumbnails from YouTube. YouTube serves both, so YouTube sees your IP address and may set cookies.

Your use of YouTube through CrateSync falls under the YouTube Terms of Service and the Google Privacy Policy.

Google Drive

Drive sync is optional. When you connect Drive, CrateSync asks Google for two kinds of access:

  • Read access to your Drive. CrateSync uses it to find and list audio files and playlist files, and to download the ones you play, cache or import. Read Tags downloads only the start of each song's file, where its title, artist and cover art are stored.
  • Access to the files CrateSync itself creates: a folder named "CrateSync Library", the audio you upload into it, and a library file named cratesync-source-of-truth.json.

The library file holds your song list, your playlists, your sync history, your device names and your folder mappings, so that your other devices can pull them. It sits in your own Drive. CrateSync changes or deletes only the files it created there, and only when you ask.

The Drive access token also stays in memory and is gone after a reload.

Song lookups

Two optional tools, the metadata lookup and the genre classifier, send details of the songs you pick to the CrateSync server: file name, folder path, current tags and length. The server passes them to Google's Gemini API, and the metadata lookup also runs a Google search through it. The server sends the answer back and keeps no copy.

To limit use, the server checks your sign-in and counts your requests in memory. It forgets the count when it restarts.

The server and the network in front of it

The CrateSync server sends the app to your browser and answers the song lookups. It stores no library data and writes no log of requests. Cloudflare and a sign-in gate sit in front of it. Like any web host they see your IP address, the time and the address you asked for, and the gate sets a cookie to keep you signed in.

CrateSync loads its fonts from Google Fonts, which also sees your IP address.

Who else gets your data

Nobody beyond the Google services named above, and those only when you use the feature that needs them. CrateSync does not sell, rent or trade data, and it does not use it for advertising.

Deleting your data and taking back access

  • On your device. Delete a song or a playlist in CrateSync, or clear the site's data in your browser to remove everything at once.
  • In your Drive. Delete the "CrateSync Library" folder in Google Drive.
  • On YouTube. A pushed playlist is an ordinary playlist on your account. Delete it on YouTube. CrateSync holds no other copy.
  • Your sign-in record. Write to cratesync@beric.ca from the address you signed in with. The record is deleted within 30 days.
  • Access to your Google account. You can revoke CrateSync's access at any time on the Google security settings page, security.google.com/settings/security/permissions.

Children

CrateSync is not meant for children under 13 and does not knowingly hold their data.
